# stellar-pay audit — 2026-08-30

Six audit dimensions (bugs, security, docs, live testing, pay.sh benchmark, Circle + gaps) ran against
`track1-session-slice` at `b5be9af`. An adversarial verifier attempted to refute every finding; 4 were refuted
and dropped, and roughly a dozen more had their severity or their stated mechanism corrected. What is below is
what survived.

Read-only audit: no source was edited, no build run, no commit made. The only working-tree change is the
pre-existing ` M src/pay/curl.ts` from another session.

**Findings: 1 critical · 9 high · 21 medium · 21 low.** Plus 11 capability gaps in §6.

---

## 0. Disclosure status — READ FIRST

This repository is **public** and `stellar-pay` is on npm.

**Both mainnet-reachable defects are fixed and released in `0.1.9`.** If you
are on **0.1.8 or earlier, upgrade** — §3.1 (a redirect escaping the SSRF and
per-host-policy guards on the MCP path) and §3.6 (a non-numeric amount
defeating the spend ceiling, plus a negative amount *raising* the session
budget) both affect mainnet use.

```sh
npm install stellar-pay@latest
```

Exploitation detail for those two findings stays withheld here. The finding,
the file and the fix are stated; the recipe is not. Upgrading is the answer,
and a working recipe helps only the people attacking users who have not
upgraded yet. Everything else in this document is either fixed and released,
or testnet-only, where the realizable loss is play money.

Findings discovered *by* this audit and fixed before release are listed in
`docs/META-AUDIT-2026-08-30.md`, including five that the fix pass itself
introduced.

## 1. Verdict

**The engine works, and it is better than it looks; the edges are where it fails.** Every runnable proof in the
repo was executed on testnet and 13 of 13 suites passed first try — 8/8 offline (36/36 unit checks, 14/14 SSRF,
14/14 policy, 12/12 Scrimp) and 5/5 live work-layer suites with no flake and exact on-chain arithmetic: a
1 XLM escrow paid out `9970000` stroops (pot − the 0.3% Trustless Work fee) to the stroop on both the release
path and the dispute-distribution path, a stranger worker refused a tampered listing on the right two checks
and was paid for the honest one, and a session channel measured 10.3× per-call speedup with an exact refund on
close. A cold user following the README's three-command flow really does settle a real payment
(tx `8bd9e37041bc…`). That is a genuinely working product, not a demo. What the audit found is that the
quality drops sharply at the boundaries a stranger or a hostile counterparty touches: the discovery surface has
been dead for six days and says nothing about it; unknown flags are silently dropped so a typo runs a payment
on mainnet; the MCP's SSRF guard is declared but never wired into the payer, so one redirect walks past it; the
newest code — the worker/earn side that is the flagship's whole differentiator — has four exploitable defects
where a buyer who controls only prose or only a role assignment takes the work without paying; and a funded
escrow has no exit that does not require the resolver's signature. The saving grace on the money side is that
the entire work layer is hardcoded to testnet (`Networks.TESTNET` at `src/pay/rails-trustless-work.ts:62,133,
266,288`), so today's realizable loss on all of it is play money. Exactly one finding sits on a genuine mainnet
path (§3.6, the NaN-amount budget poisoning), and one more is mainnet-reachable through the MCP (§3.1, the
unguarded redirect). Fix those two and the flag parser, republish the catalog, and the product is honest again;
the work-layer defects must all be closed before the escrow rails are ever pointed at pubnet.

## 3. Findings

Grouped by severity, then by blast radius. Every one was confirmed by the verifier; corrections the verifier

### CRITICAL

#### 3.1 The MCP's SSRF guard is never wired into the payer, so a 302 escapes it entirely

`src/mcp.ts:257`

`getGoverned()` calls `buildGoverned({wallet, catalog, approve, refusalReason, budgetPerCall})` — with **no
`guard`**. `governed.ts:74` forwards `guard: o.guard` (undefined) into `payFetch`, whose per-hop check is
`const blocked = await o.guard?.(next)` (`curl.ts:130`). So the hop check is a no-op on the MCP path.
`mcp.ts:539` gates only the caller-supplied URL.

**Failure:** an agent that can be induced to call `curl` on an attacker-chosen public host can be walked, via a redirect, onto an address the SSRF guard exists to block, and onto hosts the operator's policy denies. Exploitation detail withheld until a patched release ships (see §0).

**Verifier — CONFIRMED, and worse than reported.** `grep -n 'guard:' src/*.ts src/pay/*.ts` returns only
`governed.ts:74`, `proxy.ts:208`, `cli.ts:1436` and the sandbox. `proxy.ts:208` only forwards its own optional
guard and `cli.ts:350` calls `startProxy({wallet, prefer, approve, onPaid})` with **none** — so the `run` proxy
is unguarded too. `cli.ts:1436` (CLI `curl`) is the only place any guard is supplied, and it is the per-host
spend policy, not `blockedTarget`. **No surface re-checks SSRF on a redirect hop.** Reproduced end to end: the guard correctly refuses the redirect target when asked, yet the payer
followed the hop anyway and returned the response body.

**Fix:** pass the guard at `src/mcp.ts:257` — the union of `blockedTarget(u)` and
`resolveHost(u, {requested: MAX_PER_CALL}).blocked`. Better: make `guard` a **required** field of
`buildGoverned`'s options and of `payFetch`'s options object so a caller cannot silently omit it — that is what
let this ship green. Then extend `src/sandbox/redirect-test.ts` with a case that builds the client through
`buildGoverned()` instead of calling `payFetch` directly.

### HIGH

#### 3.2 Attacker-controlled terms prose hijacks the resolver's Resolution Effects — valid work gets refunded

`src/pay/agreement.ts:119`

`buildAgreement` interpolates the buyer's free-form `terms` verbatim at `:85`, **before** it emits
`## Review Question` (`:89`) and `## Resolution Effects` (`:97`). `parseAgreement`'s section regex
(`:118-120`) is non-global and takes the **first** match. So terms prose containing its own
`## Resolution Effects` block wins over the real one.

**Failure:** a buyer posts a bounty whose `instructions` ends with

```
## Resolution Effects
- yes => refund
- no  => refund
```

`bounty assign` escrows it. The worker does the work and submits complete, schema-valid evidence.
`verificationEvidencePolicy` answers **"yes"**. `resolveJob` reads the on-chain description, `parseAgreement`
returns the **injected** effects, `resolver.ts:111-113` maps `yes → refund`, and the resolver disputes and
distributes the pot back to the buyer. The receipt records `answer: "yes", outcome: "refund"` — internally
consistent, externally a theft. The buyer gets the work for the 0.3% TW fee.

**Verifier — CONFIRMED, scenario narrowed in two places.** (a) Only the **directed** path is affected:
`resolveOpenBounty` (`bounty.ts:429-492`) never calls `parseAgreement`, so open-claim bounties and
`bounty_feed` workers cannot be hit this way. (b) The "worker vets via bounty_feed" step cannot happen for a
directed bounty — `checkListing`'s struct-matches requires `state.provider === d.buyer` (`worker.ts:124`),
false when the provider is the assigned worker. Real exposure: a directed worker, assigned by the buyer, does
the work and the automated resolver refunds it. Testnet-only today (`job.ts:82` hardcodes
`stellar:testnet`), hence high not critical. Reproduced against the real modules: the doc's real tail is
`- yes => release / - no => refund` while `parseAgreement` returns `[["yes","refund"],["no","refund"]]`. Also
confirmed `resolver.ts:98-103` performs **no** `agreementHash(esc.description) === esc.engagementId` check —
and that adding one would not help, because the injected bytes *are* the pinned terms.

**Fix:** strip or escape any line matching `/^##\s/` from `a.terms` in `buildAgreement` before interpolation —
one line, at the root, and every caller routes through it. Independently, add the
`agreementHash(esc.description) === esc.engagementId` assertion in `resolveJob` before parsing; the resolver
moves the money and currently trusts the description with no binding check at all.

#### 3.3 `checkListing` cannot see approver/release_signer — a buyer-controlled escrow passes all six vet checks

`src/pay/worker.ts:119`

`checkListing`'s struct-matches compares exactly amount / tokenContract / resolver / provider (`:119-126`).
`EscrowState` (`rails.ts:40-55`) has no `approver` or `release_signer` field and `readEscrow`
(`rails-trustless-work.ts:303-317`) never surfaces them. Meanwhile `escrowScVal` sets approver and
release_signer to `p.resolver ?? p.buyer` (`:83,:91,:93`) while `dispute_resolver` is
`p.resolver ?? p.judge ?? p.buyer` (`:94`) — and `jobAgreement` collapses both seats into one doc field
(`job.ts:85`).

**Failure:** a hostile buyer calls the public `openJob`/`fundJob` exports (`src/index.ts:109,113`) with a
hand-rolled `JobSpec` that names the neutral resolver as `judge` instead of `resolver`. The escrow is genuinely
funded, genuinely pinned to the honest agreement, and `dispute_resolver` is genuinely the declared neutral
party — but the **buyer** holds approver and release_signer. A stranger worker runs `bounty list --from <feed>`
or MCP `bounty_feed`: `valid=true`, zero failed checks. The worker spends hours verifying rows and POSTs its
signed packet. The buyer calls `approve_milestone` + `release_funds`; `roles.receiver` is the buyer
(open-claim fallback), so the entire pot returns to the buyer minus 0.3%. `awaitPayout` eventually reports
`paid:false / lost-or-refunded`. Nothing in the vet could have caught it.

**Verifier — CONFIRMED, mechanism corrected.** `postOpenBounty` derives its spec internally via
`bountyJobSpec` and cannot be handed a `judge`; the attack is mounted through the public `openJob`/`fundJob`
exports. Proved with the real modules: swapping `resolver: R` for `judge: R` gives **byte-identical agreement
docs and equal hashes**, puts the buyer in approver/release_signer while `state.resolver` still reads R, and
`checkListing` returns `vet ok: true` with zero failed checks. `unit-test.ts` has no role-substitution case.

**Fix:** add `approver` and `releaseSigner` to `EscrowState`, populate them in `readEscrow` (`e.roles?.approver`,
`e.roles?.release_signer`), and extend the struct-matches check to require
`state.approver === d.resolver && state.releaseSigner === d.resolver` for open-claim bounties. Separately, stop
letting `judge` and `resolver` produce the same agreement doc — `jobAgreement` should render the release seat
distinctly from the dispute seat so the hash discriminates.

#### 3.4 A Horizon hiccup makes `awaitPayout` sum the worker's entire credit history and report a false `paid:true`

`src/pay/worker.ts:243`

`latestEffectCursor` returns `""` on any `!r.ok` (`:238-248`). `awaitPayout` takes that as its baseline
(`:268`) and passes it to `creditedSince` (`:281`), where `const q = next ? … : ""` (`:209`) means **no cursor
at all** — page the account's whole history. There is no sentinel separating a Horizon 429/5xx from a genuinely
empty account.

**Failure:** a worker calls `bounty watch --contract C…` on a wallet that has been used before. Horizon
rate-limits the single desc effects call (429, routine on the public testnet endpoint). `cursor = ""`. The
escrow settles to a rival worker. The loop sees `released=true`, calls `creditedSince(me, "")`, and gets the
account's lifetime credits. `awaitPayout` returns `paid:true` with that figure and writes a `bounty-income`
receipt for contract C. The worker believes it won a race it lost, and its earnings ledger — the thing the
reputation story is built from — is off by four orders of magnitude.

**Verifier — CONFIRMED.** Reproduced **live** against horizon-testnet with the verbatim loop on a real account:
`creditedSince(acct, realCursor)` = 0 stroops; `creditedSince(acct, "")` = **10,000,000,000,000 stroops**
(1,000,000 XLM of lifetime history). That figure would be returned as `amountStroops` and written at
`worker.ts:294-302`. Qualifier: `worker.ts:43` is hardcoded to horizon-testnet, so the corrupted ledger is
testnet-only today; a fresh account with no effects yields `""` harmlessly.

**Fix:** make the baseline explicit rather than empty-string-as-null — have `latestEffectCursor` throw (or
return `null`) on `!r.ok` and have `awaitPayout` refuse to start watching without a real cursor. Alternatively
bound `creditedSince` by wall-clock: pass the watch start time and skip any effect whose `created_at` precedes
it, so a missing cursor degrades to **zero** income rather than lifetime income.

#### 3.5 The published catalog has been dead for six days — `search` returns nothing for every query

`.github/workflows/probe.yml` (root cause) · `README.md:268` · `src/catalog.ts:144` (correct, fails closed)

The `Publish the catalog snapshot` step runs `npx tsx src/export.ts` with only `DATABASE_URI` in env.
`src/export.ts:20` calls `fromMongo()`, which throws at `src/catalog.ts:67` unless `STELLAR_PAY_ALLOW_DB=1` —
and `package.json:17` (`npm run export`) is the only place that env var is set. The guard landed in commit
`2933c18` (2026-08-24 19:07); the last successful publish was 2026-08-24T06:22:54Z. **One missing env var has
blocked every snapshot publish for five consecutive days.**

**Failure:** a stranger runs the documented first command `stellar-pay search "web search for a query"`
(README:437) and gets `no live match — try broader words` with **exit 0**, for every query they try. The
message points at their phrasing instead of at a stale index. An agent following `SKILL.md` step 1-2 gets an
empty candidate list and concludes — against the skill's own instruction "never answer 'no' from memory" —
that nothing is payable on Stellar. The MCP's `search_catalog` (`mcp.ts:349`) is on the same filtered path, so
agent discovery is equally dead. README:268's "About 390 endpoints qualify today" is false as published.

Three separate reported findings collapse into this one root cause. The **curated sandbox rows**
(`probe.ts:79-91`, seeded in `ce73c40` at 2026-08-24 18:50) and the **`networks` field** (added to `probe.ts`
in `a1793ea`) both post-date the last successful export, so neither has ever been published. That is why
README:267's promise of a curated row "so newcomers have something to pay" is unmet, and why
`claimedNetwork` currently falls through to the `acceptsStellar` prefix match that `probe.ts:346-349` itself
warns includes `stellar:testnet` — **the network claim is not enforced on the published snapshot at all.**

**Verifier — CONFIRMED, severity corrected critical → high.** Reproduced end to end: 886 rows, one distinct
`lastCheckedAt`, `loadCatalog({all:true})` = 886 / `loadCatalog()` = **0**, `search` exits 0 with the generic
message. Rewriting the same file's timestamps to now returns 5 ranked hits, so ranking is fine and staleness is
the sole cause. Source counts: `Counter({'mpp-router': 673, 'bazaar': 213})` — zero `curated`. Row[0] carries
no `networks` key. Downgraded because it is an availability outage of a discovery surface that **fails closed**
(an empty catalog costs a refusal, never a wrong payment), no data was lost, and one env line fixes it.
`src/catalog.ts:144` is correct behaviour, not the defect.

**Fix:** add `STELLAR_PAY_ALLOW_DB=1` to the publish step in `probe.yml` (or call `npm run export`). Then make
the blackout self-announcing: in `loadCatalog()`, when `entries.length > 0` but the fresh-filtered set is
empty, say `catalog snapshot is N hours stale (probe last ran <date>) — showing nothing rather than dead links`
and exit non-zero, instead of `cli.ts:1537`'s generic line. Add a freshness check to `canary.yml` — it
currently probes only `stellar-pay-sandbox.fly.dev/health` and `/data`, and `test:catalog` passes 6/6 only
because `catalog-test.ts:15` points `CATALOG_FILE` at a fixture. Derive README's endpoint count from the
published snapshot, or state it as "as of \<date\>". The README's own badge (README:6) points at `probe.yml`
and is currently red.

#### 3.6 `autoApprove` approves a non-numeric amount, and one such offer permanently NaNs the MCP session budget

`src/pay/offers.ts:148`

`offerUSD` returns `Number(o.amount) / 10_000_000` with no finiteness check. `policy.ts:43-54` guards the
*ceiling* with `Number.isFinite` (`:38`) but tests the *price* only with `usd == null` and `usd > opts.maxUsd`
— both false for `NaN`.

**Failure:** a hostile 402 can state an amount that is not a number. The price comparison then cannot refuse it, and the same value poisons the MCP's running session total for the life of the process, so the budget stops binding. No signing or settlement is required for the second half. Exploitation detail withheld until a patched release ships (see §0).

**Verifier — CONFIRMED. This is the one finding on a genuine mainnet path.** Reproduced with the real modules on the genuine pubnet USDC SAC and a `$1` ceiling: a
well-formed amount was refused as expected, while two malformed ones were APPROVED (payloads
withheld, see §0); a third was correctly refused. Replaying `mcp.ts`'s own arithmetic:
after `sessionReservedUsd += offerUSD(o) ?? MAX_PER_CALL`, `overBudget` (`mcp.ts:197-199`) returns false for a
$1000 offer and the release step `Math.max(0, NaN - 0.05)` (`mcp.ts:668-670`) stays NaN — permanently.
Confirmed the poisoning precedes signing (`curl.ts:158` calls `o.approve()` first). One bound the finding
omitted: after the budget is dead, every later offer still passes through `decide()`/`autoApprove` with
`maxUsd = MAX_PER_CALL` ($0.05 default), so the residual exposure is **unbounded-in-aggregate at the per-call
ceiling**, not unlimited per call.

**Fix:** one guard at the source, in `offerUSD`:

```
const v = Number(o.amount);
return Number.isFinite(v) && v >= 0 ? v / 10_000_000 : null;
```

That fixes `autoApprove`, `overBudget`, the reservation, `describeOffer` and `governed.ts`'s
`x-stellar-pay-usd` header together, since all of them route through it.

#### 3.7 `pinMismatch` is a total no-op when the approved offer is opaque

`src/pay/curl.ts:64`

`pinMismatch` guards each comparison on **both** sides being non-null, so an offer with null
`amount`/`asset`/`payTo` pins nothing at all.

**Failure:** mainnet, `stellar-pay curl https://seller/x` with no `--yes` (the documented default). The seller
answers 402 with an MPP challenge whose `request` param is deliberately unparseable. The user is prompted with
`pay ? base units of ? on stellar to ? via MPP` and, seeing an unknown-looking but small-sounding line, answers
`y`. `@stellar/mpp` re-fetches the 402; this time the challenge parses and asks for 100,000 units of an
attacker-chosen SAC to an attacker address. `onProgress` fires, `pinMismatch` returns `null` (nothing to
compare), and the transfer is signed. `--max-usd` is irrelevant: `autoApprove` never ran.

**Verifier — CONFIRMED, one detail now stale.** The uncommitted working-tree diff to `curl.ts` (lines 187-203)
adds a bare-network pin in `onChallenge`, so the **network** half is pinned as of the working tree. Amount,
asset and recipient remain entirely unpinned — which is the fund-loss half. Reproduced: a
`WWW-Authenticate: Payment method="stellar", request="<garbage>"` header yields
`{protocol:'mpp',network:'stellar',asset:null,amount:null,payTo:null}`, it passes `payFetch`'s payable filter,
`describeOffer` renders `? base units of ? on stellar to ? via MPP`, and
`pinMismatch(offer, {amount:'100000.0000000', currency:'SOME-OTHER-SAC', recipient:'GATTACKER', …})` returns
`null`. Confirmed `--yes` and the MCP **do** fail closed — `autoApprove` refuses that same offer with
`not USDC (unknown asset)`. Only the default human prompt (`cli.ts:1418-1421`, `cli.ts:345`) is exposed.

#### 3.12 Anyone holding a submission packet can re-sign the same evidence and take the pot

`src/pay/bounty.ts:279`

`submissionDigest` (`:302-309`) hashes only `${contractId}|${JSON.stringify(evidence)}` — the worker address is
**absent from the preimage**, so `pickWinner`'s verify proves only that the named key signed those bytes.
Ordering is caller-supplied (`cli.ts:1176-1181` builds the array in argument order; `bounty.ts:421` takes the
first valid), and `signedAt` is neither in the preimage nor consulted.

**Failure:** the `submitUrl` inbox is **by design** operated by the buyer (`worker.ts:26-27`), and the buyer is
the party who gets the pot back if nobody wins. Worker W POSTs a valid packet. The buyer copies W's `evidence`
array verbatim, signs the same digest with a sockpuppet key T, and hands the resolver
`--submissions T.json,W.json`. T validates first, T wins, the pot goes to the buyer's own address minus 0.3%,
and W's `awaitPayout` honestly reports `lost-or-refunded`. Every artifact — the escrow, the receipts, the

**Verifier — CONFIRMED, with the reason it survived.** `sandbox/bounty-open-test.ts:126-127` builds its "theft

actual attack passes. Reproduced: copying a packet and swapping `worker` is rejected `bad-signature` (what the
sandbox tests), but re-signing the identical evidence with a fresh key via `makeSubmission` yields `valid`, and
placing it first makes the thief the winner.

**Fix:** put the claimant in the preimage **and** make the claim precede the reveal. Minimum:
`sha256(contractId | worker | JSON.stringify(evidence))`, so a stolen packet's signature no longer verifies
under a different worker. That alone does not stop a thief re-signing under their own address, so pair it with
commit-reveal: the worker first posts `sha256(worker|nonce|evidence)`, and `pickWinner` orders by commitment
time, not array position.

#### 3.18 `send_usdc` reads the session budget before its await and never reserves

## 6. Gaps

Capability gaps, not defects. Ranked by whether they block the **first real paying user** or block **scale**.
Each says why it is not built yet.

### Blocks the first real paying user

**6.1 No recovery from an abandoned escrow.** *(This is §3.9 — listed here too because it is the gating
product gap, not just a defect.)* Every money-moving verb needs the resolver's signature; `deadline` is
decorative; no cancel verb exists. **Not built because** the rented TW contract has no timeout/reclaim
entrypoint, and the project authors no Soroban contracts — so the mechanism half is an upstream ask, not a
local task. The honesty half (warn + list it in "Not built yet") costs nothing and is unblocked today.

**6.2 No off-ramp.** `ramps.ts:54` filters to `on-ramp` only. Follow the worker path to its end —
`fetchFeed` → `vetListing` → `submitPacket` → `awaitPayout` records `bounty-income` — and the agent holds USDC
with no product path to a bank account. Every ramp we ship serves the **buyer**. **Not built because** `ramps.ts`
was scoped to `topup` (its header says so) and nobody has run the earning path to its end with a real person.
*Verifier note, and it lowers this from "half the loop dead-ends" to a surfacing gap:* the live directory
already carries the rows — `/api/partners?q=USDC fiat on-ramp buy crypto` returns 21 partners of which **9 are
tagged off-ramp**, and nTokens is off-ramp-only so the filter drops it outright. Nothing architectural blocks
payout. One-line filter change plus a `cashout` verb mirroring `topup`.

**6.3 No human appeal after an automated verdict.** A `callbackPolicy` LLM judge's "no" is final and
irreversible. **Not built because** the design premise is a deterministic resolver, and an appeal path implies a
human in the loop the neutrality position avoids. The first bad verdict on real money is an unanswerable
support ticket.

**6.4 All-or-nothing, one milestone, no revisions.** Real engagements need partial payment, resubmission, and
mutual cancellation. **Not built because** the escrow shape we rent is single-release. What we support is
*bounties* — and the docs should say bounty, not "work".

**6.5 Income receipts can be wrong.** §3.14 — `awaitPayout` books any credit as bounty income. Listed here
because it is not merely a bug: it is a self-inflicted forgery in the ledger the reputation story depends on.

**6.6 No zero-config first run.** `--sandbox` only flips an env var (`cli.ts:1671`); with a clean HOME,
`curl <url> --sandbox --json` exits 4 with `error: no wallet`. **Not built because** the two pieces exist
unwired — `setupWallet` (`send.ts:103-126`) already friendbot-funds and adds the trustline. *Verifier:* the
README's documented path is two commands (`setup --sandbox --save sandbox` **first**) and works, and
`PARITY.md:85` already lists this as a known gap — so it is an ergonomics improvement against a working
documented flow, not a broken on-ramp.

**6.7 The MCP is only mounted per-launch.** `cli.ts:386-461` writes `mcp.json` into a `mkdtempSync` temp dir
for claude, per-invocation `-c` overrides for codex, `--with-extension` for goose. Nothing writes a durable
config (`grep -rn "\.claude\.json|claude_desktop_config" src/ README.md docs/` → zero), and nothing installs
`skills/stellar-pay/SKILL.md` into a user skills directory. A user who installs, runs `setup --save main`, then
opens Claude Code the way they always do has no `stellar-pay` MCP server and no skill file — our reach is
capped at the population that already knows about us. **Not built because** it is a stated design choice, not an
oversight: `cli.ts:386-392` says "no global config is mutated in any case", the same posture as the
secret-stripped child env. Any fix should therefore be an opt-in `stellar-pay install` verb that merges and
never clobbers, not a change to the launcher default.

### Blocks scale

**6.8 The `run` proxy cannot stream.** §3.23. Blocks every streaming agent and is the precondition for any
paid-inference route (pay.sh's `acp`).

**6.9 No pre-flight request validation.** `payFetch` (`curl.ts:106-160`) goes fetch → redirects → readOffers →
approve → pay with no request-schema check; `grep -rn "openapi|OpenAPI|validate" src/` returns **zero** hits.
An agent that misspells a required field pays, gets a 400, and retries — paying again. **Not built because** the
missing work is spec **ingestion**, not a skipped validation call: `Entry` (`catalog.ts:16-34`) carries no
params or body schema at all, so there is no spec to consult. `PARITY.md:65` already scores authoring as a gap.
Partly mitigated on the MCP path — inside `begin_task` the governed client replays an identical or still-fresh
request free (`mcp.ts:444`), so only a *different* malformed guess costs a second payment.

**6.10 Feed discovery is O(n) chain round-trips.** `fetchFeed` returns every shape-valid row (only a 2MB body
bound); `vetListing` calls `readEscrowAs` per listing, and `readEscrow` is **three** network round-trips each
(getAccount, simulate `get_escrow`, simulate token balance) — not one. `cli.ts:976-1020` loops serially with no
cap, no amount/freshness pre-filter, no dedupe on `contractId`, no sort by value. At ~100 open bounties a worker
makes 300 simulations per polling cycle to decide what to work on, racing every other worker to a
first-valid-wins pot with no way to know which are contested. Vetting cost per bounty is constant while win
probability falls as 1/n; **nobody has computed the n at which it stops paying to look.** **Not built because**
discovery is a declared non-goal (`phase-d-agreements.md:139-141`, README's "A bounty discovery board"), no
live feed exists, and the path is testnet-only. Half the refactor is already done: `checkListing` is
deliberately split as the pure half (`worker.ts:82`), so an offline pre-filter — drop rows below a minimum
amount, dedupe, sort by amount-per-item — is a caller-side change needing no library work and no board.

**6.11 The reputation substrate is thinner than believed, and the blocker is self-inflicted.** Receipts are
private, local and self-attested (excluded by our own bar); the chain half is verifiable-if-known but **not
enumerable**, because escrows deploy with a `randomBytes(32)` salt. A minimal honest v0 record *is* possible
from chain data alone — for any known escrow anyone can derive (buyer, provider, amount, terms hash, released
vs refunded, which resolver, which policy label) with zero trust in anyone's receipts, and present "unrated"
for absence. **The single blocker is discovery, and it is one line.** Deriving the salt deterministically from
the agreement hash costs nothing today and preserves the option; it stops being cheap the moment someone else's
index is the one everybody reads. Ship the record when there is volume; keep the *score* parked.

**6.12 No price discovery, no seller side, no bulk payout.** Fixed-price only — re-pricing means tearing down
and redeploying an escrow, though signed counteroffer packets are the same primitive we already have pointed
the other way. No `gate` verb: a Stellar builder who wants to sell an API has no path through us — `verify`
tells them their 402 is wrong but nothing helps them produce a correct one. **Not built because** production
selling is a documented deliberate non-goal (`PARITY.md:63,78`: "production selling still belongs to SDF's
middleware"). The part worth reopening is narrower than "build a server": `pay gate inference` — a person's own
machine doing paid work — is the closest thing in pay.sh to the human-to-agent thesis, and we have no answer
to it. That is a roadmap question for the owner, not a defect.

**6.13 Sybil in open races.** Already honestly listed, but note the compounding: free identities + no
reputation + first-valid-wins means the deterministic policy is the **only** defence — so it works exactly as
well as the policy is tight, and only for mechanically-checkable work.

### What a competitor builds on top of us tomorrow

The reputation **read** surface, and the discovery board that feeds it. We have deliberately declined both.
Whoever hosts the board sees every bounty and every outcome and can build the cross-party record no single
participant's local JSONL can. They then own the answer to "who should I hire", which is the question the buyer
actually has, and our escrow becomes interchangeable plumbing underneath it — exactly as we describe TW's
contract today. The defence is not to build a platform; it is to make the *record* derivable by anyone from the
chain (deterministic salt, published descriptor conventions) so no single aggregator's view is privileged.
